    Over several decades, 3D reconstruction technology has achieved remarkable breakthroughs and found widespread applications in areas such as industrial inspection, autonomous driving, and cultural heritage preservation, providing high-precision spatial information support for downstream tasks like object recognition and scene understanding. However, image-based 3D reconstruction methods cannot be used in non-line-of-sight scenarios. For example, in logistics sorting and security screening, target objects are often covered or wrapped, rendering image-based methods ineffective. Although RF (radio frequency)-based 3D reconstruction methods can achieve target modeling in non-line-of-sight scenarios, existing approaches rely on complex RF systems, leading to high hardware costs and significant time overheads. Moreover, deep learning-based methods among them often face challenges such as reliance on large amounts of real-world labeled data and high transfer costs. To address these issues, this thesis proposes a method for 3D point cloud reconstruction based on signal multi-view fusion and domain adaptation. This method utilizes ultra-wideband RF signals to achieve 3D modeling of objects with single-dimensional data acquisition. Additionally, to tackle difficulties in data collection and labeling, this thesis introduces a real signal domain adaptation approach, achieving low-cost and easily transferable 3D reconstruction of objects. Specifically, the research contributions of this thesis are outlined as follows:

    Firstly, addressing the limitations of image-based reconstruction methods in occluded scenes and the high cost of RF-based methods, this thesis proposes a 3D point cloud reconstruction method based on multi-view depth inverse projection fusion of RF signals. The proposed method uses a low-cost commercial ultra-wideband radar with single-transmit-single-receive capabilities to construct a virtual antenna array for signal acquisition of objects. Background reflection reduction and effective range bin localization are applied to obtain denoised target signals. To address the issue of insufficient diversity in radar data, virtual antenna offset and phase rotation data augmentation methods are designed. Subsequently, a signal-based multi-view depth reconstruction network extracts 3D structural information from RF signals to generate multi-view depth maps, which are then converted into point clouds through depth map inverse projection and point cloud fusion. Experiments on a self-built dataset show that the Chamfer Distance (CD) error of the reconstructed point cloud using this method is 5.326 cm, with F1 scores of 62.17\% and 76.34\% at thresholds of 2 cm/4 cm, respectively. In fully occluded scenarios, the CD error of the reconstructed point cloud increases by only 0.977 cm compared to non-occluded scenarios, demonstrating the robustness of this method in occluded environments.
    
    Secondly, to overcome the dependency on large quantities of real-world labeled data and high transfer costs associated with deep learning-based 3D reconstruction methods, this thesis proposes a 3D point cloud reconstruction method based on signal domain adaptation. By meticulously modeling object reflection signals, a radar signal simulation algorithm was designed and implemented. A signal domain adaptation neural network module was also developed to reduce distribution discrepancies between simulated and real domains, ultimately creating a large-scale simulated signal dataset. Using transfer learning, the signal-based multi-view depth reconstruction network is first pre-trained on the simulated signal dataset before being fine-tuned on the real signal dataset, thereby enhancing model data efficiency, training speed, and performance. Under the proposed signal domain adaptation-based 3D point cloud reconstruction method, the CD error decreased by 0.458 cm, achieving higher point cloud reconstruction accuracy. When only 5\% of the training data from the real signal dataset was retained for fine-tuning the signal-based multi-view depth reconstruction network, the CD error was 5.175 cm, representing an increase of only 0.307 cm compared to full-data fine-tuning, significantly improving the data efficiency of the network model. Furthermore, the training cycle of the signal-based multi-view depth reconstruction network on the real signal dataset was reduced from 50 epochs to just 8 epochs, substantially lowering training costs.
