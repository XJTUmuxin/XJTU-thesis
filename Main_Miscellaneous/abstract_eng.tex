    3D reconstruction technology has made significant breakthroughs over decades of development and has been widely applied in fields such as industrial inspection, autonomous driving, and cultural heritage preservation, providing high-precision spatial information support for downstream tasks like object recognition and scene understanding. However, traditional image-based 3D reconstruction methods still have limitations in certain specific scenarios, such as logistics sorting and security inspection, where target objects are often covered by packaging materials, and environmental lighting conditions are restricted or visual occlusion exists, causing image-based methods to fail. Existing radio frequency (RF) signal-based 3D reconstruction methods mostly rely on complex RF systems with high hardware costs. Additionally, deep learning-based approaches often suffer from issues such as dependency on large amounts of labeled real-world data and high migration costs. Therefore, developing a 3D reconstruction method that combines low cost with penetration capability holds significant research value. This thesis addresses the above challenges through the following two research directions:

    First, to address the failure of existing image-based 3D reconstruction methods in visually occluded scenarios, this thesis proposes a 3D point cloud reconstruction method based on multi-view inverse projection fusion of signals. The method utilizes only a low-cost single-input single-output commercial ultra-wideband radar to construct a virtual antenna array for signal acquisition of objects. Background reflection cancellation and effective range bin localization are applied to obtain denoised target signals. To address the issue of insufficient diversity in radar data, a data augmentation method involving virtual antenna offset and phase rotation is designed. Subsequently, a 3D reconstruction neural network model extracts 3D structural information from radar signals to generate multi-view depth maps, which are then fused through depth map back-projection and point cloud integration to generate reconstructed point clouds. Experiments on a self-built dataset demonstrate that the Chamfer Distance (CD) error of the reconstructed point cloud is 5.326 cm, with F1 scores of 62.17\% and 76.34\% at 2 cm/4 cm thresholds, respectively. In fully occluded scenarios, the CD error of the reconstructed point cloud increases by only 0.977 cm compared to non-occluded scenarios, validating the robustness of this method in occluded environments.
    
    Second, to address the reliance of deep learning-based 3D reconstruction methods on large amounts of labeled real-world data and high migration costs, this thesis proposes a 3D point cloud reconstruction method based on signal domain adaptation. Detailed modeling of object reflection signals is conducted to design and implement a radar signal simulation algorithm. A signal domain adaptation neural network module is also designed to reduce the distribution discrepancy between simulated and real domains, ultimately constructing a large-scale simulated signal dataset. Transfer learning is then applied: the 3D reconstruction network model is pre-trained on the simulated signal dataset and fine-tuned on the real signal dataset to improve data efficiency, training speed, and performance. Under the proposed signal domain adaptation-based 3D point cloud reconstruction method, the CD error of the reconstructed point cloud is 4.868 cm, representing a reduction of 0.458 cm compared to training from scratch, achieving higher point cloud reconstruction accuracy. When only 5\% of the training data from the real signal dataset is retained for fine-tuning the 3D reconstruction network, the CD error of the reconstructed point cloud is 5.175 cm, increasing by only 0.307 cm compared to full-data fine-tuning, demonstrating the network's higher data efficiency. Furthermore, the number of training epochs required for the 3D reconstruction network on the real signal dataset is reduced from 50 to just 8, significantly lowering training costs.
