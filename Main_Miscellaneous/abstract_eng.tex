Three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction technology has achieved significant breakthroughs over decades of development and is widely applied in fields such as industrial inspection, autonomous driving, and cultural heritage preservation, providing high-precision spatial information for downstream tasks like object recognition and scene understanding. However, traditional image-based 3D reconstruction methods still face limitations in specific scenarios, such as logistics sorting and security inspection, where objects are often covered by packaging materials, and environmental lighting conditions are restricted or visual occlusions exist, leading to the failure of image-based approaches. Existing RF signal-based 3D reconstruction methods heavily rely on complex RF systems, resulting in high hardware costs. Therefore, developing a cost-effective 3D reconstruction method with penetration capability is of critical research significance. This paper addresses these challenges through the following two aspects of research:

First, this paper proposes a 3D point cloud reconstruction method based on Ultra-Wideband (UWB) virtual array. The method uses low-cost single-input single-output (SISO) commercial UWB radar to collect object signals and obtains denoised effective target signals through signal processing. To address the insufficient diversity of radar data, a data augmentation algorithm is designed. A neural network model extracts 3D structural information from radar signals to generate multi-view depth maps, which are then fused via inverse projection and point cloud merging to reconstruct the final point cloud. Experiments on a self-built dataset demonstrate that the proposed method achieves a Chamfer Distance (CD) error of 5.326 cm, with F1 scores of 62.17\% and 76.34\% at 2 cm and 4 cm thresholds, respectively. In fully occluded scenarios, the CD error increases by only 0.977 cm compared to non-occluded scenarios, validating the method's robustness to occlusions.
    
Second, this paper introduces a transfer learning method based on signal modeling and domain adaptation. By modeling object reflection signals, a radar signal simulation algorithm is designed and implemented. A signal domain adaptation neural network module is further developed to reduce distribution discrepancies between simulated and real-world signal domains. A large-scale simulated signal dataset is constructed, followed by a transfer learning framework: the 3D reconstruction model is first pre-trained on the simulated dataset and then fine-tuned on real-world data. This approach significantly improves data efficiency, training speed, and model performance. Under transfer learning, even with only 5\% of the real signal dataset retained for fine-tuning, the model achieves a CD error of 5.1747 cm. The training cycle is reduced from 50 epochs to just 8 epochs. Furthermore, when fine-tuned with the full real dataset, the CD error is further reduced to 4.868 cm.
